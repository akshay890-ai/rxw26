The 10 Rules of the Field

  • 1. Observe before you act. Information first, decisions second.
  • 2. Baseline, then deviation. Know what normal looks like.
  • 3. Hold hypotheses loosely. Body language is probability, not proof.
  • 4. Listen twice as much as you speak. People tell you everything if you let them.
  • 5. Keep your cover simple. The fewer the lies, the fewer the slips. Truth is the best cover story.
  • 6. Ask, don't accuse. A good question beats a confident guess.
  • 7. Slow is smooth. Calm breathing, calm voice, calm movement.
  • 8. Protect your information. Strong passwords, privacy settings, small footprint.
  • 9. Always have a backup plan and an abort condition.
  • 10. Integrity is the real cover. Trust earned honestly lasts; trust gained by deceit collapses.

Influence: How It Works and How To Defend

These six levers of persuasion appear in movies and in everyday marketing. Learn them to use fairly, and to notice when they are used on you.

  • Reciprocity: we repay favors. Defense: a gift is not a debt.
  • Commitment: we stay consistent with earlier statements. Defense: it's fine to change your mind.
  • Social proof: "everyone is doing it". Defense: check the evidence.
  • Authority: we obey titles. Defense: verify credentials independently.
  • Liking: we say yes to people we like. Defense: separate the person from the request.
  • Scarcity: "only 2 left". Defense: slow down; urgency is a red flag.
Academy ethics: influence is honest when the other person has a real choice and would still agree if they understood exactly what you were doing. If it only works in the dark, don't do it.
